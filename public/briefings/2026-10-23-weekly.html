01 · AnthropicResearch previewOct 21

Claude Duet pairs proactive work with explicit approvals

Claude Duet is a separate proactive-agent app that checks connected accounts, searches prior Claude chats, brings back sourced summaries, and pauses before sending email, paying, signing, or deleting. It is available only through a U.S. waitlist for Pro and Max users, with access opening in waves.

Restrictions: U.S.-only waitlist, Pro or Max, invitation required; Team and Enterprise are not yet supported. It runs on Opus 5.5, uses normal plan limits—including background checks—and cannot be watched or taken over in its browser. Microsoft 365 is not yet available and Slack is limited.

This is close to the approval-first cloud pattern you want for property-tour intake and inbox cleanup: collect evidence automatically, but hold consequential actions for review.

Create a read-only morning check across my connected Gmail and Drive. Report: new property-tour emails, listing-status changes, missing source files, and anything requiring my decision. Cite the exact email or file for every item. Do not send, delete, move, purchase, sign, or change anything. Group proposed actions into one approval list.

04 · xAIPublished use caseOct 20

Grok Bot shows a concrete event-monitoring pattern: detect, hold, approve, sweep

xAI described two cloud teammates: one checks the preceding 15 minutes of support tickets every five minutes for spikes, while another prepares an approved batch once an incident is resolved. In one example, a human-reviewed sweep cleared 204 related tickets in about 10 minutes.

Editorial visual · Source: xAI’s Grok Bot incident case study

This is a newly published implementation pattern, not a new entitlement. Grok Bot access still depends on your subscription or enterprise setup. The useful design is the separation between detection, temporary holding, a human-reviewed batch, and final action.

The same pattern can protect your property-brief ingestion and PBC workflows from duplicate emails, clustered failures, or partial updates while keeping one approval gate.

Design a cloud-only monitor for my property-brief pipeline. Every five minutes, review events since last_seen. Normalize each error into a fingerprint, group repeats in a rolling 15-minute window, and alert only when the same fingerprint appears three times or affects multiple listings. Hold matching updates, prepare one review batch, and require approval before publishing or emailing. Store last_seen, fingerprint, listing ID, source URL, and disposition in a Google Sheet state file.
